Introduction

This specification defines a flexible framework that describes how the machine processing of Persistent identifiers (PIDs) resolves into an FAIR Digital Object, which is consists of harmonized structure used to represent the metadata and data of any PID identified resource.

An FDO is any PID identified resource whose PID resolves to a structured FDO record compatible with this specification that provides consistent access to is metadata and data.

This specification aims to be implementation neutral while specifing a flexible building block approach that promotes a simple, consistent, machine actionabile and interoperable access to of any resource on the Internet. Any FDO specification complient client should be able to resolve any PID into an FDO, access and process its associated resource as long as the FDO is compatible with this specifiation regardless of the underlying technologies.

FDOs are required to be machine actionable as part of the specification to promote their automated processing and faciliate their access and use such as in the case of AI systems.

A more detailed description of an FDO defines it as predictable, persistent, and standardized structure that can represent (i.e., identifies and describes) any digital or physical resource of any type (data, metadata, semantic assertions, configurations, AI models, etc.) and complexity. An FDO may not necessarily contain the resource itself and simply reference it. From a resource's perspective, the FDO specification represents a method for structuring and describing its information to provide access to it. Every FDO implements the FAIR principles [[FAIR]] and complies by-default with these principles:

  • An FDO is identified by a globally unique, persistent, and resolvable identifier known as a PID).
  • A PID resolves, directly or indirectly, to a structured, machine-actionable PID record that describes the identified resource.
  • The PID record contains or has external reference(s) to the resource's data bit-sequence and associated metadata bit-sequence(s), when appropriate.
  • An FDO should include license and provenance information, and standard, domain-specific metadata.

FDOs are an implementation of the FAIR principles that can improve data sovereignty, transparency/traceability, accountability, interoparability and persistence. These characteristics are critical when establishing a stable, robust, and highly distributed ecosystem of data, which includes well-documented AI models and their training corpus. Society, businesses and governments will increasingly depend these FDO caracteristics to establish trust in data and data services. While FDOs can be passive units of information they can be directly associated with executable operations which can be used for data encapsulation and faciliatate autonomously acting agents.

FDO characteristics and properties, provide a common harmonization layer for the many existing and emerging dataspaces in science and industry. They specifically provide solutions for the trust, control and data planes of dataspace.

Origin of this document

The concept of FAIR Digital Object (FDO) emerged from meetings, in the fall of 2019 consisting of representatives from the Research Data Alliance (RDA), the FAIR community and other organizations that all recognized the potential of FAIR as well as the need to further its technical development. The FAIR community had identified that machine actionability [[Jacobsen2020]] and interoperability were a key FAIR requirements. The RDA had determined that Digital Objects (DO), as described in a seminal paper on the Digital Object Architecture (DOA) by Kahn and Wilensky [[Kahn-Wilensky]], represent a foundational core architectural component that enable machine actionable data management, and facilitate interoperability and reusability that could directly address these FAIR requirements. These two perspectives were seen as synergetic to the future development of FAIR.

These meetings resulted in an agreement between the various representatives to collaborate on the elaboration of data infrastructure technologies, the definition of FDO technical requirements and the creation of a governance structure to advance FDO development.

This FDO specification document is a result of this collaboration, under the governance of the FDO Forum, and it specifies how Digital Objects, as the core architectural component, can implement the FAIR principles and become FAIR Digital Objects. The FDO Forum is an international, independent organization that specifies FDOs and best practices, organizes the FDO community, and coordinates FDO implementations and applications. It is not yet a legal entity, therefore, it will need to be represented by an existing organization until it becomes a legal entity.
