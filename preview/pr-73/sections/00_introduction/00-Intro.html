Introduction

The concept of FAIR Digital Object (FDO) emerged from discussions, in the fall of 2019, between members of the Research Data Alliance (RDA) the FAIR Community and others organizations that recognized the potential of FAIR as well as the need to further its technical development. These discussions resulted in an agreement to converge on the elaboration of data infrastructure technologies, define FDO technical requirements and agree on a governance structure to advance FDO development. The FAIR community had clearly identified that machine actionability [[Jacobsen2020}} and interoperability were key FAIR requirements. The RDA had determined that Digital Objects (DO), as specified in a seminal paper on the Digital Object Architecture (DOA) by Kahn and Wilensky [[Kahn-Wilensky]], are a foundational, core architectural concept that enables machine actionable data management, facilitates interoperability and reusability that could directly address these FAIR requirements. This specification document is a result of this collaboration and specifies how FAIR can be implemented using the Digital Object Architecture.

An FAIR Digital Object (FDO) is a predictable, standardized and persistent bundle of information that represents (i.e., identifies, encapsulates, and describes) a digital or physical resource which can be of any type (data, metadata, semantic assertions, configurations, AI models, etc.) and complexity. Every FDO implements the FAIR principles [[FAIR]] and complies by-default with these principles:

  • An FDO is identified by a globally unique, resolvable, and persistent identifier knows as a PID).
  • A PID resolves (directly or indirectly) into a structured, machine-actionable metadata record that describes the identified resource.
  • The machine-actioanble record contains or has external reference(s) to the data bit-sequence of the resource and additional metadata bit-sequence(s), if appropriate.
  • An FDO includes at a minimum license and provenance information, and standard, domain-specific metadata.

FDOs are an implementation of the FAIR principles that also improve data sovereignty, transparency/traceability, accountability, interoparability and persistence. These characteristics are increasingly more important when establishing a stable, robust, and highly distributed ecosystem of data, which includes well-documented AI models and their training corpus. Society, businesses and governments will increasingly depend these FDO caracteristics to establish trust in their data and data services. While FDOs can be passive units of information they can be directly associated with executable operations which can be used for data encapsulation but can faciliatate autonomously acting agents.

FDO characteristics and properties, provide a common harmonization layer for the many existing and emerging dataspaces in science and industry. They specifically provide solutions for the trust, control and data planes of dataspace.
