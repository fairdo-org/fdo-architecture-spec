PIDs

[R1-1] Persistence

The term persistence is being mentioned frequently in this document, as in the realm of FDOs persistence is an important property. In regard to FDOs persistence is defined as a promise of an initiative or service provider to maintain specifications or services for a long period of time. It is up to the users to decide whether they trust such a promise.

[R1-2] Persistent Identifier

Every FDO is assigned one or more globally unique, persistent, and resolvable identifier (PID). Resolving a PID via a PID system MUST always produce an at least machine-readable structure, the PID record. The PID of an FDO is assumed to be persistent even when its data has been deleted.

[R1-3] PID Record

A PID record is an at least machine-readable structure sent back when resolving a PID. A subset of the PID record MAY contain the FDO record, refer to the FDO record, or refer to another PID. The contents of a PID record can also be arbitrary, in which case, depending on the implementation, it does not provide an FDO.

[R1-4] PID System

A system that stores and manages PIDs and their PID records is called a PID system. It MUST provide mechanisms to facilitate reliable referential stability over extensive time periods. Even after this time period, a PID MUST NOT be re-assigned to another PID record, except with negligible probability. For FDOs, we expect PID systems to provide storage, access, and referential stability of PIDs and their PID records for at least 10 years after last modification. Furthermore, it SHOULD be able to handle frequent addition and modification of PID records and provide consistent, but timely results to PID resolution requests.

[R1-5] Open PID System Specification

The PID system MUST be based on specifications that SHOULD be maintained by an independent international organization and MUST cater for a distributed solution. Such specification MUST define the assignment and resolution procedures as well as other organizational and technical requirements.

[R1-6] PID system Governance

The PID system MUST be governed by an independent, non-profit organization with global governance and proven sustainability for long periods.

[R1-7] Global PID Resolution

The PID system MUST support global resolution and therefore global uniqueness of the PIDs. It SHOULD provide a high degree of consistency and/or availability. Resolution of the PIDs and therefore access to the PID Records MUST be made possible by a well-defined access protocol with open specification.

[R1-8] Security of PID Systems

The PID system MUST offer a high degree of security that only enables the owner of aFDO Record to manipulate it. It SHOULD be possible to restrict the access to the whole record or to individual key-value pairs. PID systems SHOULD fulfill the security properties confidentiality, integrity, availability, and authenticity.
